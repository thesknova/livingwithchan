## A Letter from Chan

Dear friend,

You picked up this book because something in you is asking for more.

Maybe you just turned forty and realized the plan you were following was never really yours. Maybe the kids are getting older and you are wondering what the next chapter looks like. Maybe you are starting over after a divorce, a layoff, a loss, or twenty years of putting everyone else first. Maybe you simply looked at your bank account, then at your calendar, and thought: there has to be another way to do this.

I want you to know something before you read a single page further.

You are not behind. You are not too late. And you are not starting from zero, even when it feels like you are.

I know this because my family arrived in Canada with nothing. Not "nothing" as a figure of speech. Nothing. We escaped a communist country, lived in a refugee camp in Thailand for more than a year, and landed in Calgary in 1980 because a generous family and a church we had never met decided to sponsor strangers. Everything I wore growing up was homemade or secondhand. My first business was a lemonade stand run through a chain-link fence.

And yet, by the time I was twenty-one, I had put together my first real estate deal with none of my own money and no credit. By thirty, the rental properties my partners and I had built paid me more every month than my full-time job did. I have spent more than twenty-five years investing in real estate, helping families buy and sell homes, helping homeowners facing foreclosure walk away with their dignity, and coaching women who were sure they had missed their window.

They had not missed it. Neither have you.

This book is three things at once.

It is a story. My story, and my parents' story before mine, because we are all products of the courage of those who came before us.

It is a set of lessons. Everything the banks and the schools never taught me about money, partnership, and finding out what you are actually worth.

And it is an invitation. At the end of every chapter you will find a few questions. They are not homework. They are the same questions I had to sit with at my own kitchen table, and I promise they will take you somewhere if you let them.

This is not a book about getting rich quickly. It is a book about becoming, slowly and surely, a woman who cannot be stopped.

Pour yourself something warm. Let's begin at the river.

With love and lemonade,

**Chan**

Calgary, Alberta

## How to Read This Book

Read it in order the first time, because the story only makes sense the way it happened: the crossing, the current, the partners, and then your turn.

Keep a pen nearby. The "Your Turn" questions at the end of each chapter are the working heart of this book. Women tell me the questions did more for them than the chapters did, and I take that as a compliment.

And when you finish, pass it on. Give it to your sister, your best friend, the woman at work who keeps saying "someday." This book was always meant to travel from one woman's hands to another's.
